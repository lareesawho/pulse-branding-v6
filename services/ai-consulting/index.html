The real problem

AI is easy to demo. Reliability is harder.

A clever answer is not the same thing as a dependable system. The difficult questions begin after the demo.

What does the system know?

The documents, pages and records it is allowed to read — and where that reading stops.

What does it remember?

What persists between conversations, for how long, and who can see or remove it.

What can it touch?

Each connected system is a decision about read, write and what stays out of reach.

What requires approval?

What runs without asking, what waits for a person, and what is never allowed.

How is success checked?

The system has to know the difference between an answer and a correct answer.

When does it stop?

The point where the system hands the decision to someone accountable.

System anatomy

A trustworthy agent is designed as a chain of constraints.

01
Context
What it knows

The documents, pages and records the system is allowed to read, and the boundary where that reading stops.

02
Memory
What persists

What is kept between conversations, for how long, and who can see or remove it.

03
Tools
What it can access

Each connected system is a decision: what it may read, what it may write and what stays out of reach entirely.

04
Permission
What it may do

Actions are separated into what runs without asking, what waits for a person, and what is never allowed.

05
Action
What it changes

Every write is named, logged and reversible where the underlying system allows it.

06
Verification
How success is checked

The system has to be able to tell the difference between having answered and having answered correctly.

07
Escalation
When a person takes over

The point where the system stops guessing and hands the decision to someone accountable.

The exception, designed in

Escalation is not a failure state. It is the part of the design that decides what the system is allowed to be wrong about.

Not a screenshot

Use it.

The interesting part of an agent is not the reply. It is the state around the reply: what it used, what it was allowed to do, and where a person had to agree.

Sales agentContent engineCustomer service
User

We are getting traffic but almost nobody books.

System

Before recommending more acquisition, I would inspect the point where interest becomes commitment. Do people understand the offer, see enough proof and know what happens after they click?

User

Then tell me what is broken.

System

I can audit the public pages and show you where confidence breaks. I cannot contact anyone or change your CRM until a person releases that action.

User

We need a month of content without sounding like everyone else.

System

I will work from the positioning, the customer objections and what you have already published, so the first draft argues a point rather than filling a calendar.

User

Who signs it off?

System

Nothing publishes itself. Drafts and schedules are prepared; a person approves every send.

User

Our support inbox is the same twelve questions.

System

Then the job is retrieval and routing, not conversation. Answer what the knowledge base genuinely covers, and escalate the rest with context attached.

User

What if it gets something wrong?

System

That is why it cites its source, and why anything financial, contractual or medical goes to a person immediately.
